## Launch of the Global Advocacy Data Hub

The <a href='https://globaladvocacydatahub.org' target=_blank>Global Advocacy Data Hub</a> (GADH) was originally created by advocates focused on the Global Fund's accountability to communities living with and affected by HIV, tuberculosis, and malaria.  The GADH was created in response to the <a href='https://globaladvocacydatahub.org/resources/RISE%20Report%202024.pdf' target=_blank>RISE study</a>, a global research study of community engagement with Global Fund Country Coordinating Mechanisms (CCMs).  This study, and its resulting advocacy roadmap, identified the need to engage advocates in decision-making spaces, equip them with resources and information, and empower them to advocate with decision-makers. The <a href='https://globaladvocacydatahub.org/about' target=_blank>GADH was created to meet this need</a> by providing advocates with the tools and evidence they need for their work.

Although the GADH has been showcased in several spaces, including an in-person introduction held at the International Conference on AIDS and STIs in Africa (ICASA) in December 2025, the GADH held its official launch at the AIDS2026 conference in Rio de Janeiro in July 2026.

![Img](https://globaladvocacydatahub.org/images/blog/launch-of-the-global-advocacy-data-hub-and-community-quilt-at-aids2026-2.png)

The AIDS2026 conference was an exciting opportunity to introduce the GADH to the HIV advocacy community, led by GADH members Victor Adula (<a href='https://consolation.or.ke/' target=_blank>Consolation East Africa</a>), Lizzie Otaye, Alana Sharp (<a href='https://dataetc.org/' target=_blank>Data Etc</a>), Ángela León Cáceres (<a href='https://women4gf.org/' target=_blank>Women4GlobalFund</a>), Patrick Drake (Data Etc), Jennifer Sherwood (<a href='https://www.amfar.org/' target=_blank>amfAR</a>), and Elise Lankiewicz (amfAR). During the launch event, the team introduced GADH’s objective of making information more accessible to communities and advocates, to support advocacy with donors, governments, and other stakeholders for accountable health systems. 

The team also presented a <a href='https://globaladvocacydatahub.org/resources/Position%20Statement%20by%20CSOs%20and%20Community%20Groups%20for%20AIDS2026.pdf' target=_blank>global civil society statement</a> – developed by the GADH with <a href='https://impactsante.org/' target=_blank>Impact Sante</a> and <a href='https://globalfundadvocatesnetwork.org/about/gfan-africa/' target=_blank>GFAN Africa</a> – outlining community priorities ahead of the conference. This was an effort to ensure the voices of civil society and community groups who were not physically present were represented by those who had managed to travel to Rio. The statement set out 5 key asks, which were crucial advocacy points in Brazil

![Img](https://globaladvocacydatahub.org/images/blog/launch-of-the-global-advocacy-data-hub-and-community-quilt-at-aids2026-3.png)

The meeting discussed the importance of community and civil society data for advocacy, highlighting the need for accessible, user-friendly data from sources like WHO and the Global Fund. Participants emphasized the role of community and civil society organizations in integrating data for effective advocacy and the challenges of funding and sustainability.  The GADH’s most recent reports and products were presented, including the <a href='https://dataetc.org/projects/ccm' target=_blank>dashboard of CCM data</a>, the Msingi study on integration and transition (<a href='https://bit.ly/Msingi' target=_blank>participate in the study here</a>!), and data on <a href='https://dataetc.org/projects/reprioritization' target=_blank>activities removed from grants</a> during the Global Fund’s Grant Cycle 7 reprioritization exercise. The conversation also touched on the need for government data to complement Global Fund data and the importance of community and civil society participation in decision-making and advocacy efforts.

After these presentations, participants shared reflections about the need for data and information for advocacy.  These included the need to share resources and ensure advocates have the training to use them; funding and sustainability challenges for civil society; the need for government data to be made available as donors withdraw and the role of primary healthcare systems increases; and more.  Some noted the need for community and civil society voices to have meaningful engagement in decision-making, the importance of creative approaches to advocacy, and the refusal to go back and lose the progress we have made.  Others raised the importance of localised and direct funding for communities and civil society.

## The launch of the community quilt

After the presentation of the GADH, a new community quilt was officially launched to the public.  This quilt builds on the long history of community quilts for AIDS advocacy and is only the second year in which a quilt has been produced entirely in Africa. Maxx Boykin, campaign manager of the <a href='https://savehivfunding.org/' target=_blank>Save HIV Funding</a> campaign, shared remarks about the history of quilts from the beginning of the HIV response.  During the session, the GADH presented how the quilt was made by local artists in Nairobi, and afterward every participant in the meeting and at the conference was given an opportunity to sign the quilt and add their own advocacy messages through art.

![Img](https://globaladvocacydatahub.org/images/blog/launch-of-the-global-advocacy-data-hub-and-community-quilt-at-aids2026-5.png)

Since AIDS2026, the quilt has continued its journey. After returning to Kenya, a team of advocates took the quilt to Dar es Salaam, where it has taken a round to different organizations in COMPASS Africa and those working in health and HIV access.  The quilt will continue its journey to get more signatures and arts ahead of the <a href='https://uscha.life/' target=_blank>United States Conference on HIV/AIDS</a> (USCHA)  with which it will be able to continue with the Save HIV Funding campaign.

In addition to the GADH launch, the team was active throughout the AIDS2026 conference.  GADH members co-hosted with Women4GlobalFund an official workshop on <a href='https://women4gf.org/2026/07/24/navigating-gc8-through-a-feminist-lens-a-new-w4gf-guide-for-advocates/' target=_blank>equipping communities with the resources they need</a> to advocate in Grant Cycle 8, launched initial findings from the Msingi study, presented on the findings from the <a href='https://www.amfar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/PEPFAR-Pulse-Study_2448.pdf' target=_blank>PEPFAR Pulse</a> study, held a session on triangulating data sources to measure disruption due to donor withdrawal, presented several posters, and participated in sessions on social contracting, artificial intelligence, and more. These opportunities presented advocacy entry points for the Global Advocacy Data Hub in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil.
